## 1. A) austere — *Easy*

The colon signals that what follows *defines* the blank. "Leave large areas of the
surface untouched" = plain, unadorned, stripped back. **Austere** is precisely that.

- **B) vibrant** — describes colour or energy, not the density of detail. The contrast
  drawn in the sentence is crowded vs. empty, not dull vs. bright.
- **C) chaotic** — this describes the *early* work ("crowd the frame with detail"),
  so it reverses the sentence's direction.
- **D) derivative** — means unoriginal, a judgement about influence. Nothing in the
  text concerns originality.

**Strategy:** predict your own word before reading the options. If you predicted
"empty" or "bare", you would eliminate three options without deliberating.

---

## 2. B) A feature once considered a defect turns out to explain its durability — *Medium*

The text's structural pivot is `however`. Old explanation (volcanic ash) → new finding
(the lime chunks, formerly read as sloppiness, are a self-healing mechanism). B captures
that reversal.

- **A)** — this is the explanation the text *supersedes*. Reporting a rejected hypothesis
  as the main idea is the single most common Central Ideas trap.
- **C)** — true but far too narrow. It reports one detail and omits the significance,
  which is what makes it the main idea.
- **D)** — an overreach. The text says the chunks were *previously dismissed* as careless
  mixing, not that careless mixing improves concrete.

**Strategy:** the main idea almost always lives on the far side of `however`, `but`,
or `yet`.

## 3. B) Rose from 22% to 81%, then fell to 38% — *Hard*

The conclusion has **two** parts: it peaks **and** it declines. The correct answer must
evidence both. Only B traces the full arc.

- **A)** — supports only that 10 °C is lowest. Says nothing about a peak or a decline.
- **C)** — a valid comparison, but 15 °C vs. 25 °C skips the peak entirely; it is
  consistent with a simple downward trend.
- **D)** — establishes the peak but stops there. Without the fall to 38%, the data are
  equally consistent with "rises and then plateaus".

**Strategy:** count the claims in the conclusion. Two claims require two data points
in the answer. This one rule resolves most quantitative-evidence questions.

## 4. B) decades; — *Medium*

Both halves are independent clauses ("The archaeologist has excavated…" / "her work has
reshaped…"). A semicolon is the correct boundary.

- **A) decades,** — comma splice. A comma alone cannot join two independent clauses.
- **C) decades** — run-on (fused sentence). No punctuation at all.
- **D) decades, which** — `which` must modify a noun, but here it would have to introduce
  a clause that already has its own subject (`her work`). Ungrammatical.

**Strategy:** cover the blank and ask *"can each half stand alone as a sentence?"*
If yes to both, you need a period, a semicolon, or a comma **plus** a conjunction.

---

## 5. B) includes — *Easy*

The subject is **collection** (singular). `of manuscripts` is a prepositional phrase
and can never contain the subject.

- **A) include** / **C) have included** / **D) are including** — all plural forms,
  agreeing with `manuscripts` instead of `collection`.

**Strategy:** mentally delete every prepositional phrase, then read the subject next to
the verb: *"The collection … includes."*

---

## 6. B) Consequently — *Medium*

Cause: birds perceive UV light. Effect: plumage looks different to them than to us.
This is a result relationship.

- **A) Nevertheless** — signals contrast against expectation. The second sentence follows
  from the first; nothing is being conceded.
- **C) For example** — the second sentence is a *consequence*, not an instance of
  UV perception.
- **D) In contrast** — the strongest distractor, because the passage does contrast humans
  with birds. But that contrast sits *inside* the first sentence ("a capacity humans
  lack"); the relationship *between* the two sentences is cause and effect.

**Strategy:** name the relationship in your own words first — "so", "but", "for instance" —
then match. Never pick a transition by how sophisticated it sounds.

---

## 7. C) Along with goods such as silk and spices… ideas and religions — *Hard*

The stated goal is **non-material** transmission. Only C mentions ideas and religions.

- **A)** — accurate, but addresses the network's structure and geography.
- **B)** — lists exclusively material goods, the opposite of the goal.
- **D)** — addresses duration.

**Strategy:** Rhetorical Synthesis is not a reading question. All four options are
factually true. Read the **goal sentence first**, underline its key term
(*"not material goods"*), and eliminate on that term alone.

An unanswered question and a wrong answer score identically, so leaving a blank is
always a strictly worse decision than guessing.
