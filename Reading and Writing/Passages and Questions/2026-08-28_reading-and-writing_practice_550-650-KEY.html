Per-question breakdown

1. A) skeptical — Craft and Structure · Easy

"Only after three years of regrowth did she describe herself as genuinely optimistic" tells you she was not optimistic before. The word must name that earlier doubt.

  • B) enthusiastic — reverses the arc; enthusiasm is the later state, not the initial one.
  • C) indifferent — indifference is the absence of an opinion, but the sentence frames a change of belief driven by evidence, which implies prior doubt, not prior neutrality.
  • D) bewildered — means confused, not doubtful; nothing in the sentence is puzzling her.

Predict your own word before reading options. If you predicted "doubtful," you eliminate three choices without deliberating.

2. C) self-critical — Craft and Structure · Medium

The colon defines the blank: she "candidly documents the miscalculations that derailed her signature legislation." Recording your own errors frankly is being self-critical.

  • A) self-aggrandizing — the exact opposite, and "rather than burnishing her legacy" rules it out explicitly.
  • B) evasive — contradicts "candidly."
  • D) partisan — about political loyalty; not what the sentence measures.

3. D) It presents a historical fact that complicates conventional accounts — Text Structure and Purpose · Medium

The sentence begins with "But" and delivers a fact (Korean movable type) that the last sentence explicitly says "complicate[s] the familiar story."

  • A) — the text never refutes the Korea claim; it endorses it.
  • B) — the text says nothing about whether Korean type spread.
  • C) — the author has no argument here to concede a weakness in; the passage is expository.

4. B) By contending that the gains are genuine but likely to fade — Cross-Text Connections · Hard

Oyelaran's finding is that early gains "faded within eighteen months." He does not deny the gains happened; he denies they last. That is a challenge to Feld's word "durably."

  • A) — Text 2 never comments on interruptions; that detail is only in Text 1.
  • C) — neither text disputes the commuting-time effect.
  • D) — too strong. Oyelaran describes an eighteen-month decline, not an immediate loss.

For cross-text response questions, name the precise point of disagreement first ("permanent vs. temporary"), then find the option that matches it.

5. B) The axolotl retains juvenile features into adulthood, a trait valuable to science — Central Ideas and Details · Easy

Two sentences: (1) the axolotl keeps larval traits and skips metamorphosis — neoteny; (2) this has made it a favored research subject. B combines both.

  • A) — "only amphibian" is an overreach the text never claims.
  • C) — habitat is background detail, not the main idea.
  • D) — the text describes the axolotl's neoteny, not a disadvantage for other species.

6. C) "The final 1967 text is three lines, cut from a thirty-line 1924 version" — Command of Evidence (textual) · Medium

The claim is about the scale of revision. Thirty lines down to three is direct, quantified evidence of a drastic cut.

  • A) — about her career length, not this poem's revision.
  • B) — critics' disagreement speaks to reception, not to how much changed.
  • D) — her general care with syllables says nothing about one poem's revision history.

Underline the exact claim ("bears little resemblance to the original"). The right evidence attacks that claim's key term — here, the amount of change.

7. D) The share fell steadily from 71% to 44% as commute length rose — Command of Evidence (quantitative) · Hard

The conclusion has two parts — highest at the shortest commute and a steady decline. Only D traces the full trend across all four rows.

  • A) — supports only "highest at the shortest commute"; says nothing about the decline.
  • B) — reports one endpoint; consistent with many patterns, not just a steady decline.
  • C) — shows one step down (63% → 52%), not the whole trend.

Count the claims in the conclusion. Two claims need an answer that supplies data for both.

8. A) serves the same purpose that growing toward light serves later — Inferences · Medium

The passage says growing toward darkness "reliably leads the seedling to a surface it can climb toward the canopy." Reaching the canopy is exactly the goal phototropism serves in a mature plant — so the behavior is functional, just via a different cue.

  • B) — the text calls the behavior "reliable," the opposite of an error.
  • C) — water stress is never mentioned.
  • D) — directly contradicts "leads the seedling to a surface it can climb toward the canopy."

9. B) predecessor, and — Boundaries · Easy

Both halves are independent clauses ("The telescope can detect…" / "it catalogued…"). Two independent clauses join with a comma plus a coordinating conjunction.

  • A) predecessor, — comma splice; a comma alone cannot join two independent clauses.
  • C) predecessor — fused sentence (run-on), no punctuation at all.
  • D) predecessor; and — a semicolon already does the joining; adding "and" is redundant and non-standard.

Cover the blank: "can each side stand alone?" If yes to both, you need a period, a semicolon, or a comma + conjunction.

10. C) allows — Form, Structure, and Sense · Medium

The subject is "array" (singular). "Of strain sensors mounted along the bridge's underside" is a prepositional phrase and cannot contain the subject.

  • A) have allowed / B) allow / D) are allowing — all plural, agreeing with "sensors" instead of "array."

Mentally delete every prepositional phrase, then read subject next to verb: "the array … allows."

11. A) had been climbing — Form, Structure, and Sense · Medium

"By the time the expedition reached the summit" is a completed past event. The nineteen hours of climbing happened before that past point, so you need the past perfect progressive.

  • B) has been climbing — present perfect; wrong time frame for a narrative set entirely in the past.
  • C) climbs — simple present; no time relationship to the past reference point.
  • D) is climbing — present progressive; same problem.

12. D) However, — Transitions · Medium

Sentence 1 lists advantages (lighter, corrosion-resistant). Sentence 2 gives a disadvantage (expensive, limited use). The relationship is contrast.

  • A) For instance — sentence 2 is not an example of the alloy's advantages.
  • B) Therefore — the high cost is not a consequence of being light and corrosion-resistant.
  • C) Similarly — the two sentences point in opposite directions, not the same one.

Name the relationship in plain words ("but") before looking at the options. Never pick a transition by how sophisticated it sounds.

13. C) Though Voyager 1 was built to study Jupiter and Saturn, it has since traveled into interstellar space — Rhetorical Synthesis · Hard

The goal is to emphasize change since launch. Only C sets the original purpose against the current situation ("Though … it has since …").

  • A) — pairs launch date with a still-true fact; no sense of change.
  • B) — states the current status but omits the original mission, so there is nothing to change from.
  • D) — describes only the starting point.

Rhetorical Synthesis is not a reading question — every option is factually true. Read the goal sentence first, underline its key idea ("has changed"), and eliminate on that alone.

14. A) To argue that turning penicillin into a usable drug involved far more than Fleming's observation — Text Structure and Purpose · Hard

The whole paragraph pushes against the "lucky accident" story: "would have led nowhere without" Florey and Chain; "the product of a laboratory program, not a lucky glance."

  • B) — the text never discusses how penicillin acts on bacteria.
  • C) — the text credits Florey and Chain; it does not accuse them of anything.
  • D) — the 1928 dish is mentioned in one clause, not described.
