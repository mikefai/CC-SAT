The Core 100

The Digital SAT does not ask you to define words in isolation — that was the old SAT. It asks Words in Context questions: which of four close synonyms is the precise word for this exact sentence. These 100 words are the ones that keep showing up in that role.

Part 1

How this is actually tested

A Words in Context question gives you a sentence with a blank and four options that are all in the same general neighborhood of meaning. You are not choosing the "hardest" word — you are choosing the one whose precise shade of meaning fits this sentence. That's why every card below includes an example sentence, not just a definition: the sentence is the actual skill.

How to study this list

Don't try to learn 100 words this week. Work through one category (10–13 words) at a time, spaced across several short sessions, and mix in review of an earlier category before adding a new one. A crammed list you forget in a week is worth less than 30 words you actually own.

For the teacher

How to run this

  1. Spread it over 8–10 sessions, one category per session (10–13 words), 10–15 minutes of flashcards followed by that category's share of the cloze/quiz items. Do not attempt all 100 in one sitting.
  2. Interleave old categories. From session 3 onward, open with a two-minute flip-through of a category from two sessions ago before starting the new one — this is what actually moves words into long-term memory, not re-reading the list.
  3. Frame it correctly for students: this is not "SAT vocab to memorize," it is training for Words in Context questions specifically. Every drill should be done as "which word fits this sentence," never "what does this word mean" alone.
  4. Homework: the reference table's 12–13 words from that session's category, self-tested with the card covered. One category, not the whole page.
